## What does “ISP proxy” mean?

An ISP proxy is an IP address associated with an internet service provider, often presented as a residential connection. It sits between traditional residential proxies and data-center proxies in terms of how websites classify the traffic.

A data-center proxy is normally fast and easy to scale, but some websites can identify large blocks of data-center IPs quickly. A residential proxy is associated with a consumer ISP network and may appear more like traffic from a home connection. An ISP proxy combines some of those residential characteristics with more stable hosting and performance.

For practical purposes, HypeProxies’ ISP plans are designed around three characteristics:

1. **Static endpoints**
   The IP does not rotate automatically every few minutes. This can be useful when a session, account, or workflow benefits from a consistent address.

2. **Unlimited bandwidth**
   The public plans are priced by the number of IPs and billing period rather than by gigabytes used. That is helpful for data-heavy tasks, provided the use complies with the provider’s terms and the target website’s rules.

3. **US-based availability**
   HypeProxies’ main ISP product pages emphasize US locations. That is useful for accessing US-facing services, testing US results, or running workflows that require American IP addresses. It is less suitable if you need a broad international location list.

## How to evaluate the proxies before scaling

Do not judge a proxy provider only by its advertised bandwidth or headline speed. Run a controlled evaluation against the websites and software you actually plan to use.

Check the following:

1. **Connection stability**
   Test whether connections remain open for the length of your sessions.

2. **Response time**
   Measure latency from the server where your automation runs, not only from your home connection.

3. **Success rate**
   Track completed requests, timeouts, CAPTCHA frequency, and HTTP errors.

4. **IP consistency**
   Confirm whether the address remains stable for the required period.

5. **Authentication compatibility**
   Test the supplied IP-and-port format with your browser automation tool, scraper, or proxy manager.

6. **Target-site acceptance**
   A proxy that works well with one website may perform poorly with another.

7. **Replacement process**
   Find out how to request help if one or more IPs are unsuitable.

A small test is usually more useful than a generic speed claim. It tells you whether the product works for your exact task.
